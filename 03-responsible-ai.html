Day 3 · Responsible AI principles

Week 1 · Foundations · ~45–60 min

Learning goals

Understand recurring responsible-AI principles and recognize that they can conflict.

Recurring principles

Accountability: identifiable people remain responsible. Transparency: stakeholders get meaningful information about AI use. Explainability: relevant reasons can be understood at an appropriate level. Fairness: unjustified disparities are identified and addressed. Privacy: personal data is handled appropriately. Safety & robustness: systems perform reliably. Human oversight: humans have an appropriate role, authority and information.

Principles conflict

Transparency can expose security information. More data can improve subgroup testing while increasing privacy exposure. Safety filtering can suppress legitimate expression. Human review can reduce automation harms while adding inconsistency and worker-wellbeing risks.

Governance is not a checklist of virtues. It is a documented way to resolve trade-offs in context.

Exam habit: Be suspicious of absolutes such as “always require a human” or “maximize transparency.” Good governance is proportionate.

Apply it

A moderation model reduces abuse exposure but has higher false positives for reclaimed slurs. Which principles are in tension? What evidence do you need before changing it?
