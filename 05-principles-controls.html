Day 5 · Principles → controls

Week 1 · Foundations · ~45–60 min

Learning goals

Translate policy language into controls with owners, triggers, procedures and evidence.

Policy is not a control

“Our AI must be fair” is an objective. A control is concrete: defined systems must test specified metrics across relevant groups before deployment; results meet thresholds or receive documented risk acceptance; evidence is retained and testing repeats after material change.

Anatomy of a control

Objective: what risk is reduced? Scope/trigger: when does it apply? Owner: who performs it? Activity: what happens? Threshold: what is acceptable? Evidence: what proves it? Frequency: when repeated? Escalation: what happens on failure?

Preventive, detective, corrective

Preventive: deployment gates, access restrictions. Detective: monitoring, QA sampling, complaint analysis. Corrective: rollback, retraining, disabling features, remediation.

Evidence matters

A governance program should show what decisions were made, by whom, using what evidence and what happened when thresholds were missed. Documentation is operational infrastructure.

Consulting test: Ask “What would I expect to see if this were genuinely happening?” That points to controls and evidence.

Apply it

Policy: “High-impact AI systems must have meaningful human oversight.” Turn it into three controls. Specify owner, trigger and evidence.
